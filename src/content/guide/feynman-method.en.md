# Master Any Topic with the Feynman Technique

The **Feynman Technique** is a mental model named after Nobel Prize-winning physicist **Richard Feynman**. Known as the *"Great Explainer"*, Feynman believed that true understanding means being able to explain a complex concept in plain, simple language.

---

## 🧠 The 4-Step Feynman Algorithm

### 1. Choose a Specific Concept
Select the topic you just learned (e.g., *Database Indexing*, *TCP Handshake*, or *Active Voice in Grammar*).

### 2. Explain it to a 12-Year-Old
Pretend you are teaching this topic to a beginner who has no prior background. 
- Avoid jargon, buzzwords, or textbook definitions.
- Use simple analogies and everyday language.

### 3. Spot Your Knowledge Gaps
When you get stuck, stumble, or resort to complex technical terms, **you have found a knowledge gap**. 
- In Multitrack, mark these points as **Gaps**.

### 4. Review & Refine
Go back to your source material to clarify what you missed. Re-write your explanation until it feels completely natural and effortless.

---

## 📝 Example: Good vs. Bad Explanation

- ❌ **Passive / Jargon Heavy**: *"Database indexing utilizes B-Tree data structures to reduce the I/O operations required for query optimization."*
- ✅ **Feynman Simple**: *"Indexing a database is like creating an alphabetical index at the back of a textbook. Instead of reading every page to find a topic, you jump straight to page 142."*
